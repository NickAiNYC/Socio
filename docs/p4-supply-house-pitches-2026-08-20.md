## Kamco Supply Corp

**What they sell:** Over 10,000 SKUs across 12 categories — acoustical ceilings and suspension systems, wood and metal specialty ceilings, lumber/plywood/decking, light-gauge steel framing and C-joists, structural floor panels, drywall and FRP panels, drywall forms, commercial doors and frames, insulation, flooring, jobsite protection and safety, tools and firestopping.

**Who their customer is:** Contractors and builders, general contractors, single and multi-unit residential developers, and both small and large commercial projects. **Interior/drywall-weighted, not a lumber-first yard** — that shapes the pitch.

**Existing contractor programs:** Yes, and this matters — they already run a **credit account program** for contractors, plus volume discounts and contractor pricing. They deliver next-day (sometimes same-day), offer boom service up to 10 stories, spider forklifts, Saturday delivery at select locations, and a second afternoon run.

**Read on their competitive pressure:** Kamco isn't fighting Home Depot for these customers — Home Depot doesn't boom-lift drywall to the 8th floor. They're a specialty distributor competing on availability, delivery logistics, and credit. Their real exposure is **construction volume itself**: if their contractors aren't closing work, order volume drops and the credit book gets riskier. That's the lever.

### The 60-second pitch — Kamco

> "Buenas, jefe. Sesenta segundos y lo dejo trabajar.
>
> Ustedes le dan crédito a contratistas. Eso significa que cuando a un maestro se le seca la obra, ustedes son los que se quedan esperando el pago — no solo dejan de vender, sino que la cuenta se les pone lenta.
>
> Yo soy Nick, de Socio. Nosotros le metemos obras de remodelación directo al WhatsApp de los maestros hispanos de Brooklyn y Queens. Ellos no nos pagan nada por adelantado — solo cobramos cuando el cliente les deposita el anticipo.
>
> Un maestro con obra cerrada compra material y paga a tiempo. Ese es su negocio, no el mío.
>
> Lo que le propongo: deje estas veinte tarjetas en el mostrador, con su nombre atrás. Cada contratista que usted mande y cierre obra, le pasamos el 1% del contrato — entre $400 y $1,200 por obra, directo a usted.
>
> ¿Le dejo el primer paquete aquí junto al POS, y le tomo el WhatsApp para mandarle el primer reporte?"

**The "why should I trust you" move is in the second sentence:** you named their credit exposure — a problem a random marketer would not think of. That's the 15-second credibility buy.

**Ask for by name:** the pro desk / counter manager, or whoever handles contractor accounts. Not the front desk.

## Feldman Lumber (US LBM)

**What they sell:** Lumber, drywall, board products, general building supplies, and specialty products. The Brooklyn location is a **full-service lumber yard *and showroom*** — the showroom matters (see below). The Great Neck location adds a paint store.

**Who they are corporately:** Feldman is a long-standing Brooklyn lumber name, now part of **US LBM**, a national building-products distributor. Practical implication: **the counter guy may not have authority to approve a referral program**, and anything smelling like a formal partnership may route to a regional manager. Pitch the counter guy as an *individual* earning a bounty, and be ready to be told it needs a sign-off.

**Hours (Brooklyn):** Mon–Fri 7am–5pm, Sat 7am–2pm; call center until 6pm weekdays. **Go between 9:30 and 11am** — the 7–9am contractor rush is over and the lunch run hasn't started.

**Read on their competitive pressure:** A Brooklyn lumber yard with a showroom is squeezed from two sides — big boxes on commodity lumber price, and the fact that under US LBM they're measured on volume. The showroom is the tell: they're trying to capture the *homeowner-facing* end of jobs, not just sell studs to whoever walks in. That's a genuine shared interest with you, and it's a different pitch than Kamco's.

### The 60-second pitch — Feldman

> "Buenas. Un minuto y lo suelto.
>
> Ustedes tienen showroom aquí, o sea que les interesa el trabajo de remodelación completo, no nada más vender madera al que llegue. Ahí es donde yo les sirvo.
>
> Soy Nick, de Socio. Le llevamos obras de remodelación a los contratistas hispanos de Brooklyn — directo al WhatsApp. Ellos no pagan nada hasta que el cliente les deposita el anticipo, así que cualquier maestro puede entrar sin riesgo.
>
> Cada contratista que agarra obra con nosotros viene por su material a algún lado. Yo prefiero que sea aquí — y si usted me manda maestros, le pasamos el 1% de cada contrato que cierren, entre $400 y $1,200 por obra.
>
> No le pido nada firmado hoy. Le pido su WhatsApp, y le mando el primer maestro esta semana para que vea cómo funciona. ¿Le parece?"

**The trust move here is different from Kamco's:** you read their *showroom* as strategy rather than furniture, which tells them you looked. And the close deliberately asks for **less** — no signature, no counter space, just a phone number and one referral — because a US LBM employee probably can't say yes to a program on the spot, but he can absolutely say yes to a WhatsApp number.

## Tactical notes for both visits

| | Kamco (506 Morgan Ave, Brooklyn) | Feldman (1281 Metropolitan Ave, Brooklyn) |
|---|---|---|
| **Angle** | Their credit exposure to slow contractors | Their showroom / whole-job ambition |
| **Ask** | Card display + WhatsApp | WhatsApp + one referral, no commitment |
| **Likely blocker** | "Talk to the branch manager" | "That has to go through corporate" |
| **Counter-move** | "Perfecto — ¿cuándo está? Vuelvo." Get a name and a time. | "Entonces esto queda entre usted y yo. Usted manda, usted cobra." |

**Bring:**

- 20 cards per location, with a blank space on the back for **their** name and Zelle
- Your phone open to `socio.nyc/contratistas` — showing beats describing
- The one-page pilot agreement, in case a contractor is standing at the counter (they will be — that's half the reason to go)

**Do not:**

- Ask for a customer list. Instant no, and you'll never get a second conversation.
- Say "partnership" or "referral program" in the first 30 seconds. Say "le paso una comisión."
- Pitch during the 7–9am rush. You'll get the brush-off you deserve.

**The real reason to go is the contractors in line, not the counter guy.** Two supply-house conversations might yield one bounty partner. But standing at that counter from 9:30 to 11 puts you next to a dozen Hispanic GCs who are, by definition, actively buying material for active jobs — the highest-intent prospect list in Brooklyn, and it's free. Plan for that. Bring enough agreements.
